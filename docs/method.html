How 5-day chlorophyll-a composites become the cell sums this site is computed from.

Same method as trend.sst

trend.chl is the third deployment of grist, after trend.sst and trend.sla. The summarization is identical: every source pixel is assigned whole to the S2 level-5 cell containing its centre, nothing is warped or resampled, and each cell keeps four sums per composite (n, sumw, sumwx, sumwx2) from which the mean and variance of any union of cells and dates follow exactly. The full explanation, with figures and references, is on the trend.sst Method page.

The source

ESA Ocean Colour CCI version 6.0: merged multi-sensor L3S chlorophyll-a, 5-day composites on the 1/24° (about 4 km) geographic grid, from September 1997. The composites run every 5 days without restarting at the new year. A level-5 cell holds roughly 45,000–60,000 pixels. The files are read as cloud-optimized GeoTIFFs from Pawsey object storage; each records the original file and checksum it was converted from.

This is a fixed release, so the record ends where the release ends and nothing on the Status page is provisional.

Log space

Chlorophyll-a is close to lognormal: concentrations span four orders of magnitude, and a plain mean is dominated by a few blooms. grist applies a named transform before summing, here log10, so every sum is of log10(chl-a, mg m-3) and every cell mean is a geometric mean (−1 is 0.1 mg m-3, 0 is 1 mg m-3). Each sums file records its transform. Trends are per decade in log10 units: +0.1 per decade is about a 26% rise per decade.

The product also provides per-pixel uncertainty in log10 space (chlor_a_log10_rmsd, chlor_a_log10_bias). They are not used yet; the RMSD would give inverse-variance weights as a second set of sums.

Ocean cells only

OC-CCI keeps inland water, so many cells over lake-rich land (Canada, Siberia, northern Europe) have values. Every series, map and mode on this site uses only the cells the OISST ocean record has, minus the Caspian, the Great Lakes and Ladoga/Onega.

Gaps are expected

Ocean colour needs daylight and clear sky. At high latitudes the polar winter leaves whole months without data (on 1 May there is nothing south of about 55°S), and cloud thins the record everywhere. A cell with no valid pixels in a composite has no row for it; nothing is filled in.
